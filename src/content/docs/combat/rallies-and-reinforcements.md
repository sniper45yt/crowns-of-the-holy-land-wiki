## Reinforcements

A guildmate can send troops to an ally's castle like a normal march. The troops then **stay there** to defend it, until you recall them home.

- While stationed, they fight as part of the castle's army in **every** attack on it.
- They lose troops like the owner does, and the losses are stored in the **sender's own** Hospital until it is full. See [Casualties and the Hospital](../casualties-and-hospital/).
- The sender gets the battle report.
- Sending costs a march slot. How many troops a castle can host is set by its **Embassy**, 200 per level.

## Rallies

A rally lets guild members attack together as one army.

1. A guild member starts a rally against an enemy castle (or to reinforce an ally).
2. Guildmates join with their troops during the **join window**, chosen by the leader: 5, 10, 20 or 30 minutes.
3. The combined force launches, either early by the leader or automatically at the deadline, and marches at **half speed**.
4. On arrival all participants' troops fight as **one army** against the castle's army.

A rally's size is limited by the leader's **War Hall**, 200 troops per level.

### After the battle

- Each participant's losses are their own: 20% wounded into their own Hospital, the rest dead. Survivors return to their own garrison.
- Kills are split by the damage each participant's troops dealt.
- All loot goes to the leader.
- The leader's attack research applies to the whole attacking force.

### Who can see a rally

The leader, the participants and the leader's whole guild can see it. So can the **target and the target's whole guild, from the moment it opens**. They see how many troops are committed and joined, but never who brings what, so they can prepare reinforcements.

### Disbanding

Before it launches, a rally can be cancelled by its leader or by any **R4 or R5 member of the leader's guild**. Everyone's troops are refunded.
